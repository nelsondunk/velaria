My pick if Saira is out: Manrope, with IBM Plex Sans as the runner-up. Manrope keeps the clean, machined feel but its bowls are round, so letters stop looking like squares, and its tall lowercase stays readable at 20 px. Plex is the most legible of the five and reads like an engineering instrument; it is a little plainer. Barlow is the closest to road signs and camera menus (DIN family), narrow enough to fit more on the strip, but it is almost as square as Saira. Red Hat Text is the friendliest and the least "pro tool".

My pick: Polished. It reads as metal because of the bright band near the top and the darker band just below the middle, the way a curved copper surface reflects a room. Satin is the safe, quieter option. Brushed looks best here but its fine lines are finer than a headset pixel, so in the headset they turn into shimmer; I'd keep it for the website and print. Whichever you pick, text on it stays dark ink and passes 4.5:1 at its darkest band.
