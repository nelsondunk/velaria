You can't judge a sphere on a flat monitor.

A flat screen can't show depth, so you can't tell how far away a title sits or whether it will float in the viewer's face. So checking one change goes like this:

  1. Export.

    Render the sequence to check one title.

  2. Sideload.

    Copy it to the headset and find the file.

  3. Squint.

    The horizon leans and the title sits too close.

  4. Again.

    Headset off, nudge a value, guess, repeat.

Fix it while you're in there.

Point at a title, a footage window or the whole picture, and change it from inside the shot. Every move is a real change in your Resolve project, with undo.

  • Place titles and footage

    Point at an overlay in the scene to select it, then drag it where it belongs. Pan, tilt, roll, distance and scale each have their own control.

  • Set stereo depth by eye

    Push a title back or pull it forward and watch it settle against the scene, so nothing ends up floating in the viewer's face.

  • Level and re-orient 360

    Turn on the level grid for a true horizon, then re-level the shot or turn the sphere so the action starts in front.

  • Keyframes and undo

    A keyframe on any control, marks on the timeline deck, and undo that goes straight to your Resolve project.

  • Always know what you're moving

    Overlays, patches and the whole picture are separate layers, each named, with a lock so the plate stays put.

  • Scopes in the headset

    Waveform, RGB parade, vectorscope and histogram, measured on your Mac from the frame Resolve sends, before any compression.

  • A trim pass on the delivery device

    Exposure, lift, gamma, gain, contrast, saturation, temperature and tint on a layer above your grade. A trim for the headset your audience will wear, not a final grade.

  • It tells you what's wrong

    When a Resolve setting is off, the headset names it and gives the fix in Resolve's own menu words.

Questions, answered straight.

If yours isn't here, write to us before you buy. We would rather answer a question than process a refund.

What do I get when I buy Velaria?

One download for your Mac. It contains the Velaria output that DaVinci Resolve Studio plays to, the Velaria View app for your Quest, the Velaria effects for Resolve (Canvas Control, Orient 360 and Trim), an installer and uninstaller, and the setup guide.

It is a one-time purchase with no subscription and no licence key.

Which Mac, which Resolve and which headset do I need?

A Mac with Apple silicon running macOS 13 or newer, DaVinci Resolve Studio 19, 20 or 21, and a Meta Quest 3. Quest 3S, Quest 2 and Quest Pro run the app, but Quest 3 is the headset we test on.

You also need a USB-C data cable for the install. After that you can work over the cable or over Wi-Fi.

Does it work with the free version of DaVinci Resolve?

No. The free version has no video output for Velaria to connect to, so Velaria needs DaVinci Resolve Studio.

Why does my Quest need developer mode?

Velaria View is not in the Meta Horizon Store yet, so the installer loads it onto your headset over the cable. Meta only allows that when developer mode is on. Turning it on means creating a free Meta developer account, and Meta may take a few days to verify the account.

If you are not comfortable doing that, please wait for a store version before you buy.

macOS says it can't verify the developer. Is that expected?

Yes. Velaria 1.0 is not signed or notarized by Apple. On most Macs the installer runs from Terminal without a warning. If macOS does stop it, the requirements box above gives the exact message and the fix: System Settings, Privacy & Security, Open Anyway, then run the installer again.

I own a DeckLink or UltraStudio. Can I keep using it?

Yes, but not at the same moment in version 1.0. Resolve sees Velaria as a Blackmagic video device, so while Velaria is installed it takes the place of Blackmagic Desktop Video. The installer will not set Blackmagic's driver aside unless you tell it to, and the uninstaller puts it back.

Having both listed side by side in Resolve is in the works and will be a free update.

Does it work with Premiere Pro, Final Cut Pro or on Windows?

No. Velaria 1.0 is for DaVinci Resolve Studio on a Mac.

Does it work with Apple Vision Pro, Pico or other headsets?

No. Velaria 1.0 is for Meta Quest. If you work on Apple Vision Pro, DaVinci Resolve Studio has its own monitoring for that headset.

Which formats and resolutions can I preview?

360 in mono or over/under stereo, and 180 as side-by-side VR180 or as dual fisheye with lens presets from 144 to 220 degrees.

Pictures up to 5760 pixels wide reach the headset at full size (a 5760 x 2880 VR180 timeline, for example); wider pictures are scaled down to 5760. Resolve's monitoring picture is no taller than 4320 pixels, so a square 360 timeline such as 5760 x 5760 is monitored at 3840 x 3840 while you work. The headset tells you when that setting is needed.

Can I finish a colour grade in the headset?

No. A Quest is not a reference display. Velaria gives you scopes measured on the Mac and a trim pass, so you can check and adjust how the film sits on the device your audience will wear. Grade on your reference monitor.

Cable or Wi-Fi?

Both work, and the app finds your Mac on its own. The USB-C cable is the steadier choice. Over Wi-Fi, the Mac and the headset need to be on the same network, and a fast, uncrowded one.

How is this different from a headset app that shows a video stream from my Mac?

Those apps show you a picture. Velaria also stays in step with Resolve's own viewer, frame for frame, and it lets you change the project from inside the shot: move an overlay, set its depth, level the horizon, set a keyframe. Every change is a real change in your Resolve project, with undo.

When is dome support coming, and what will it cost?

Dome preview is planned for version 1.1 as a paid add-on for people who make fulldome and planetarium work. We have not set a date. Version 1.0 does not include it.

How many computers can I install it on? Are updates free?

One purchase covers one person, on the Macs and headsets that person uses. A second editor needs a second copy. Every 1.x update is free. A later major version may be a paid upgrade.

Does Velaria send any data anywhere?

No. There is no account, no analytics and no telemetry. The picture travels between your Mac and your headset over the cable or your own network, and nowhere else. The privacy statement has the details.

What if it doesn't work for me?

Write to us within 14 days of buying and we will refund you, no questions asked. Support is by email. If you run the log script that comes with Velaria and attach the file, we can usually see what went wrong.
