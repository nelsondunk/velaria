You can't judge a sphere on a flat monitor.

A flat screen can't show depth, so you can't tell how far away a title sits or whether it will float in the viewer's face. So checking one change goes like this:

  1. Export.

    Render the sequence to check one title.

  2. Sideload.

    Copy it to the headset and find the file.

  3. Squint.

    The horizon leans and the title sits too close.

  4. Again.

    Headset off, nudge a value, guess, repeat.

Install. Headset on. Press play.

Fix it while you're in there.

Point your controller at a title, a footage window or the whole picture, and change it from inside the shot. Every move is a real change in your project, with undo.

  • Place titles and footage

    Point at an overlay in the scene to select it, then drag it where it belongs. Pan, tilt, roll, distance and scale each have their own control.

  • Set stereo depth by eye

    Push a title back or pull it forward and watch it settle against the scene, so nothing ends up floating in the viewer's face.

  • Level and re-orient 360

    Turn on the level grid for a true horizon, then re-level the shot or turn the sphere so the action starts in front.

  • Keyframes and undo

    A keyframe on any control, marks on the timeline deck, and undo that goes straight to your Resolve project.

  • Always know what you're moving

    Overlays, patches and the whole picture are separate layers, each named, with a lock so the plate stays put.

  • Panels you arrange

    Placement, Look and Timeline panels sit where you leave them. Turn off the ones you don't need.

  • Scopes in the headset Building

    Waveform, RGB parade, vectorscope and histogram, measured from the uncompressed frame your editor sends.

  • Colour trim pass Planned

    Exposure, lift, gamma, gain, saturation and contrast on a trim layer above your grade. A trim on the device your audience will use, not a final grade.
